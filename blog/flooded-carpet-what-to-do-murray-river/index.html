Floodwater in Your Carpet? What to Do in the First 48 Hours

By Ultraclean Swan Hill · 28 September 2026 · 6 min read

Flooded carpet: what to do along the Murray River comes down to one rule: get the water out and the airflow on within 48 hours. After that, the underlay starts to smell, and mould can take hold in the carpet, the underlay and the bottom of the plasterboard. Whether the water came from a burst pipe in Swan Hill, an overflowing washing machine in Kerang, or the river rising through the floor in Murray Downs, the steps below are the order to do things in. When in doubt, call Ultraclean on 0417 327 173 and start the drying while you sort out the rest.

Is it safe to walk on flooded carpet?

Only once the power to that part of the house is off and you know where the water came from. Switch off the circuit at the switchboard before stepping onto wet carpet if any power points, extension leads or appliances are in the water. If the water is from the river, a sewer or a septic overflow, treat it as contaminated: wear boots and gloves, keep children and pets out, and do not try to clean it yourself. Clean water from a pipe, hot water service or appliance is safe to deal with straight away.

What should I do first when carpet floods?

  1. Stop the source. Turn off the water at the meter for a burst pipe, or the isolation tap for an appliance.
  2. Move furniture off the wet area or put foil or plastic under the legs. Timber and metal legs stain carpet within hours.
  3. Lift rugs and anything on the floor — boxes, cables, curtains touching the carpet.
  4. Photograph everything before you start removing water. Insurers want to see the extent of the damage.
  5. Call a water damage cleaner so extraction and drying equipment is on the way while you do the next steps.
  6. Extract as much water as you can with a wet vacuum if you have one. A mop and towels barely touch the underlay.

Can flooded carpet be saved?

Clean water: usually yes, if it is dried within one to two days. River, storm or sewage water: usually no. Water damage is classed in three categories. Category 1 is clean water from a supply pipe or appliance — carpet and often underlay can be dried and reused. Category 2 is grey water from a washing machine or dishwasher — carpet can often be saved but the underlay is normally replaced. Category 3 is black water from the river, a sewer, or any water that has sat for more than a couple of days — carpet and underlay are removed and the subfloor is sanitised.

Along the Murray between Swan Hill, Nyah and Tooleybuc, floodwater that comes up through the floor is category 3 regardless of how clean it looks, because it carries soil bacteria and whatever was upstream. The priority in that case is a safe strip-out and a sanitising clean so the house is ready for the rebuild.

How do professionals dry a flooded carpet?

By extracting, lifting, and forcing air through the underlay — not by leaving a fan on the surface. Ultraclean's process for water damage in Swan Hill and Lake Boga is:

  • Truck-mounted extraction to pull the bulk of the water out of carpet and underlay
  • Carpet lifted at the edge so air movers can blow directly across and under it
  • Underlay dried in place if it is clean water and caught early, otherwise removed and replaced
  • A commercial dehumidifier to take the moisture out of the air so the room actually dries rather than staying humid
  • Moisture readings taken in the carpet, subfloor and bottom of the walls every day until they match a dry room
  • Antimicrobial treatment, then the carpet is relaid, steam cleaned and deodorised

Most rooms are dry in two to three days. Leaving a household fan on wet carpet in a closed room does not dry the underlay and is the most common reason a "dried" carpet starts to smell a week later.

Will insurance cover flooded carpet?

Usually yes for burst pipes, appliances and storms; river flooding depends on your policy. Most Australian home and contents policies cover sudden escape of water from pipes, hot water services and appliances, including the drying and cleaning. Flood from a river or creek is covered only if your policy includes flood cover, which along the Murray is worth checking now rather than after the event. Ultraclean provides photos, moisture readings and a drying report so a claim can be lodged without back-and-forth, and can start the drying before the assessor has visited — early drying is what the insurer wants too.

Flooded carpet: what to do in the first 48 hours, in order

If you only read one section, make it this one. For flooded carpet, what to do in the first two days is the same whether you are in Swan Hill, Lake Boga, Kerang or across the river in Murray Downs:

  1. Hour 0–1: power off if needed, stop the source, photograph everything, call for extraction.
  2. Hour 1–4: furniture off the carpet, rugs and belongings lifted, as much water extracted as possible.
  3. Hour 4–24: carpet lifted, underlay assessed, air movers and dehumidifier running, windows closed so the dehumidifier can work.
  4. Day 2: moisture readings checked; underlay replaced if it is not drying; insurer notified with photos.
  5. Day 3–4: readings match a dry room, antimicrobial treatment, carpet relaid and steam cleaned.

What should I not do?

  • Do not turn on the ducted heating or an evaporative cooler to "dry it out" — you will spread the moisture through the house.
  • Do not put newspaper or towels down and walk away; they hold the water against the underlay.
  • Do not use a domestic carpet shampooer on floodwater; it adds water and does not sanitise.
  • Do not wait for the assessor before starting the drying. Document it, then act.
